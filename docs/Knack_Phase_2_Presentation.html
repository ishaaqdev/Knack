Introduction

  • Overview: Knack is a web-based, gamified career simulation that prepares engineering students for campus placements: a group discussion, five interview rounds, joining, an internship, an offer and full-time work.
  • Current Scenario: Placements are decided in a few conversations, yet students rarely get to rehearse a group discussion or an interview under realistic conditions.
  • Relevance: Communication, situational judgement and knowing how a specific company hires are what decide a shortlist.
  • Status at this stage: The platform is fully implemented and running. A student picks a company, takes a mock before every round, joins a discussion with five profiled panellists, speaks answers aloud, and keeps practising offline.

Problem Statement

The Core Problem: Placement-preparation tools are either static question banks that never adapt or chatbots that adapt to nothing, and both usually need a network.

Key Issues:

  • No Group-Discussion Practice: A discussion needs five or six people willing to act as candidates, so students cannot rehearse one alone.
  • Generic Preparation: Question lists say nothing about how a particular company hires or what it tests.
  • Assessment Before Teaching: Mock tools score answers without first showing what a strong answer looks like.
  • No Memory of the Learner: Every session starts from zero, so practice does not build on itself.
  • Network Dependence: AI tools stop working on a weak hostel network or an exhausted free quota.

Objectives

  • Simulate the Full Placement Path: Group discussion, five interview rounds, joining, internship, offer and work in one journey, each stage unlocked on competency and tied to real company profiles.
  • Teach Before Testing: Adaptive, AI-generated scenarios and an MCQ mock interview before every real round, with every option graded and explained.
  • Realistic Group Discussions: Five profiled characters who speak one at a time, with a live reading of the mood of the room.
  • Habit and Measurable Progress: XP, levels, streaks, stars and a level map, with per-topic skill scores and a Professional Readiness Score.
  • Personalisation Saved in the Backend: Stored profile, history and rolling statistics choose questions, difficulty and panel pressure, at a bounded prompt cost.
  • Natural and Always Available: Voice input, human-sounding AI text, and graceful degradation to a full offline mode.

Research Gap

  • Fragmented Practice: Group discussion, interview and workplace practice are offered separately, if at all.
  • Generic Employers: Preparation rarely depends on the company the student is aiming at.
  • Assessment Before Teaching: Mock tools score without first explaining what a strong answer sounds like.
  • Opaque or Absent Personalisation: Adaptation is either missing or hidden from the student.
  • Network Dependence: Generation and speech services assume a connection and a quota.
  • Generic Generated Text: Model output carries stock phrasing that students quickly learn to ignore. No reviewed work combines a profiled group discussion, employer-specific preparation, mock-first teaching and an offline mode.

System Design — Offline-First Request Path

1A session cookie is resolved to a user before any route runs.
2Gates are checked on the server: a real round returns HTTP 409 until its mock is complete.
3Offline mode is on if KNACK_OFFLINE is set (then it cannot be switched off in the app) or if the stored setting says so.
4Local sources: the 84-item question bank, the scripted panel and a deterministic rubric.
5One attempt per model down the chain, inside a time budget.
6Any failure or malformed reply drops to the same local path.
7Generated and scripted text both pass the same post-filter.
8Statistics are written for personalisation, and anything not generated is labelled, for example “Scripted panel”.

Implementation Details — Mock Interview and Company Data

  • Mock format: five multiple-choice questions before each real round; four options scored 9, 6, 3 and 1, labelled Strong, Decent, Weak and Costly.
  • Teaching first: every option is explained after the answer. Options are shuffled on every run, and scores are released from the server one question at a time.
  • Gate: the real round returns HTTP 409 until its mock is complete; XP is paid only for the first mock of a round.
  • Question bank: 84 hand-written items on 49 topics, with items for service, product, big-tech and start-up employers.
  • Company profiles: 16 employers (13 real, 3 invented) with description, culture, headcount and turnover tagged by year, hiring steps, what is tested and tips.

Or = round2( N · λt · st,r )

Openings for role r: N headcount, λt yearly intake rate (services 7%, product 6%, big-tech 2.5%, start-ups 20%), st,r share of the role. Labelled simulated; real vacancies are on the company's careers page.

Implementation Details — GD and Sentiment Analysis

  • Panel: Rohit (Dominant), Anjali (Knowledgeable), Vikram (Aggressive), Sneha (Diplomatic) and Imran (Quiet), each with a background, strengths, a tell and a way of speaking.
  • Turns: they speak one at a time. The student speaks to the room or to one person, or stays quiet; never speaking caps every score at 25.
  • Pressure: ν = (c + m) / 2 from confidence and communication: gentle below 42, tough above 66, steady otherwise.
  • Sentiment analysis: lexicon-based. Each of the last six lines is matched against word lists for pushback, agreement and bridging, and the counts give the room's mood. No AI is needed, so it works offline.
  • Faces: each panellist's expression follows their last line: pushing back, agreeing, bridging or probing.

T = 15 + 16nc + 4nq − 9nw − 8nb

W = 35 + 14nw + 14nb − 9nc

E = 25 + 8k + 5nq + (ℓ − 12)

Tension, Warmth, Energy, 0–100. nc, nw, nb: pushback, agreement, bridging lines; nq: questions; k: lines; ℓ: mean words per line.

Implementation Details — Personalisation

Every mock answer, discussion score and interview round writes one score from 0 to 100 to the training_stats table. Everything below is arithmetic on stored rows, so it works with no AI model.

  • Rolling score: the mean of the last five scores per topic; the trend compares the newer half of that window with the older half once four exist.
  • Question selection: unseen questions, weak topics and questions missed a while ago are weighted up; questions answered Strong or seen in the last six hours are weighted down.
  • Panel pressure and next step: read from the same rows, and shown on the Profile page as “What Knack has learned about you”.
  • Learner summary: a 60-word summary rewritten every three attempts when the AI is available; the prompt size stays constant.

w(q) = max( 0.15, 1 + 2·[unseen] + 1.5·[weak topic] + δ(q) )

δ: +1.5 if missed over 12 h ago · +0.3 if missed recently · −0.8 if last answered Strong · −0.6 if seen in the last 6 h

This is not RAG: there is no vector store or retrieval step. The final draw is random, so two students with the same history do not get the same five questions.

Implementation Details — Offline Mode, Voice and Humanised Text

  • Offline switch: KNACK_OFFLINE=1 or a stored setting. The model client stops at once and each feature uses its local source: question bank, scripted panel, deterministic rubric.
  • Labelled honestly: anything that was not generated is tagged in the database and labelled in the interface, for example “Scripted panel”.
  • Voice input: the Web Speech API fills the answer box as the student speaks. In offline mode the mic asks for on-device recognition, and typing always works.
  • Clear errors: a “network” error is explained as the browser's speech service being unreachable, not the student's internet.
  • Humaniser: removes markdown and praise openers, swaps stock connectives (“Furthermore” → “Also,”, “utilize” → “use”) and rewrites dashes.

Limitations and Assumptions

  • Company figures are rounded values from public disclosures and should be checked against the companies' own reports before they are quoted.
  • Openings are modelled. The intake rates and role shares are assumptions, which is why every openings figure is labelled simulated.
  • The mock bank has 84 items, so a student who retakes a round many times will see repeats.
  • The sentiment analysis is lexicon-based. It misses irony and negation: “I don't disagree” still counts as pushback.
  • Voice input depends on the browser's speech service. Firefox has none and Brave blocks it; typing always works.
  • No study with students has been run yet. The claim is that the system works as designed and offline, not that it improves placement outcomes.

Future Enhancements

  • Pilot study with students. Rate group discussions and interviews before and after, by raters who do not know the condition, and measure usability.
  • Spoken replies. The interviewer and the panel speak back; dictation exists, speech output does not yet.
  • A larger mock bank and company catalogue, with more employer-specific questions.
  • AI-based sentiment analysis for the room mood that handles negation and irony, with the lexicon method kept as the offline fallback.
  • Richer learner models behind the same question-selection interface.
  • Several companies at once and a native mobile client. The interface is already responsive, so this is incremental rather than architectural.

Conclusion

  • Objectives met. A working career simulation takes a student from a group discussion through five interview rounds to full-time work, tied to real company profiles.
  • Teaching comes first. Every real round is preceded by a mock in which every option is explained.
  • The room feels real. Five profiled panellists speak one at a time beside a live reading of the room's mood.
  • Personalisation is stored and visible. Rolling statistics in the database choose questions and pressure, and the Profile page shows them.
  • It works anywhere. Voice input, humanised text and a full offline mode; 37 of 37 checks passed offline with responses of about 1 ms.
  • Limitations are acknowledged. No student study yet, and in simulation the selection rule buys focus rather than faster learning.
